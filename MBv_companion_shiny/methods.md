### Pseudobulk Methods Details

Following spot level quality control, our dataset consisted of an RNA expression matrix for 535,248 SRT spots (observations) and RNA 28,965 genes (features).

#### Step 1: Spot-level annotation

Biological domains from the dlPFC were annotated with two parallel strategies. [PRECAST](https://github.com/feiyoung/PRECAST) spatial clustering was used to define spatial domains (domain~SP~) that mirrored canonical dlPFC cytoarchitectural lamina. This strategy identified spatially contiguous regions with low UMI counts that clustered separately and were removed from downstream analyses. Domain~SP~ consisted of the following annotations: layers (L) L1, L2, L3/4, L5, L6, and white-matter (WM). 

The second annotation strategy used existing snRNAseq data ([SZBDMulti-seq dataset](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.adi5199)) and [Seurat label projection](https://satijalab.org/seurat/reference/transferdata) to define cell type-driven domains (domain~CT~) that captured enriched signatures for cell types that often span across canonical dlPFC lamina. Domain~CT~ consisted of the following annotations: microglia and vascular cells (M/V), astrocytes (Ast), L2/3, L4, inhibitory neurons (Inb), L5, L6, and oligodendrocytes (Olg). It is important to note that these labels reflect spot-level transcriptional patterns that are enriched in the corresponding cell types and are not direct assessments of the abundance of the described cells within the SRT spots.

#### Step 2: Pseudobulk processing

Pseudobulk datasets were generated for both the domain~SP~ and domain~CT~ annotation sets by aggregating the raw counts within each annotated domain within each donor. Prior to counts matrix normalization, we performed gene filtering. We removed genes from the mitochondrial genome (which helps with normalization) and we also removed some lowly expressed genes. However, at this stage many lowly expressed genes were retained because they were abundantly expressed in the dlPFC snRNAseq dataset we used (SZBDMulti-seq). This was necessary to obtain enrichment *t*-test statistics in our SRT pseudobulk that we used to compare with snRNAseq.

Quality control (QC) was then performed on pseudobulk samples, and afterward datasets were re-normalized since the removal of samples during QC slightly impacts normalization factor calculation and subsequent logcounts. 

After pseudobulk processing the number of pseudobulk samples differed between domain~SP~ (n= 690) and domain~CT~ (n=962) annotation strategies owing to the different number of domains labeled per donor (domain~SP~ *k*=6, domain~CT~ *k*=8). Using [edgeR](https://rdrr.io/bioc/edgeR/man/filterByExpr.html) to filter out lowly expressed genes produced different gene filters for the two annotation strategies, owing to the calculation of minimum expression across the different sets of domains. Still, lowly expressed genes abundant in snRNAseq were retained. The 21,080 genes that passed these initial filters for both domain~SP~ and domain~CT~ pseudobulk datasets (and the resulting logcounts) are what are available for plotting in the “Plot” tab. 

#### Step 3: Differential expression (DE) pre-processing

Prior to DE modeling, we removed genes that were lowly expressed in SRT but had been retained due to high expression in snRNAseq. This was accomplished by implementing [edgeR](https://rdrr.io/bioc/edgeR/man/filterByExpr.html) filters and taking the intersection of genes that passed minimum expression criteria based on donor-level and domain-level assessment. For genes that passed the filtering criteria, raw counts were used as input for DE models.

Despite violin plots of gene expression being available for 21,080 genes, only the subset of genes included in DE modeling have statistics available. In lieu of *F*-test statistics, genes that were not included in DE models will appear with the disclaimer “gene not run in model, excluded by edgeR filters”. Again, owing to the differences in domains, a small number of genes were included in the domain~SP~ DE models and not the domain~CT~ DE models, and vice versa.

#### Step 4: DE modeling

[Limma](https://bioconductor.org/packages/release/bioc/html/limma.html) linear models for DE require raw count input and utilize a different method to account for library size discrepancies than [edgeR](https://rdrr.io/bioc/edgeR/man/calcNormFactors.html). Therefore, the logcounts we display in the violin plots of gene expression are not identical to the expression values used in the DE models. The logcounts values used in these plots and the expression values that the statistical model “sees” are different in a greater way. We use several covariates in both the whole-tissue and domain-restricted model designs that serve to remove gene expression variability due to these named covariate influences, isolating the changes in gene expression attributable to our independent variables. Although it is possible to regress out the influence of the covariates and produce an approximation of the expression patterns that the statistical model “sees” (called residualized expression values), for the purpose of visualizing DE results we chose to use the un-altered logcount expression. Lastly, since the RNAseq information from each of our donors was stratified into different pseudobulk samples based on the domain annotation, we utilized the [duplicateCorrelation](https://rdrr.io/bioc/limma/man/dupcor.html) function to mimic the random effects introduced by repeating measurements from each donor. This too changes what the model “sees” when fitting gene expression but is impossible to represent visually when plotting.

We implemented two different linear models for each annotation strategy. The **whole-tissue model** tested for diagnosis-by-sex differences across the entire tissue section while adjusting for differences in expression due to domain annotations (`~ diagnosis-sex group + domain + other covariates`). The **domain-restricted model** evaluated diagnosis-by-sex differences within individual spatial domains (`~ diagnosis-sex-domain group + other covariates`). For each model, we evaluated gene-level significance of both the omnibus *F*-test (to test for differences in means across all groups) and *t*-test (to test for differences between two groups as a single contrast). Therefore in our study differentially expressed genes (DEGs) required an *F*-test adj. *p* < 0.05 and a *t*-test adj. *p* < 0.05.
